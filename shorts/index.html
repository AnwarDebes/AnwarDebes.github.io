Zero attacks got through my AI agent guardrail. Here's the catch

clausegate reads every tool call an AI agent wants to run as three graphs (action, context, policy) and answers allow, deny or needs a human, with a signed receipt. No attack was approved, but only 30% of honest calls were approved outright; the rest went to a human. On tools it had never seen, every call went to a human: zero again, but this time all deferral, no detection.

Scope: a train/test split of AgentDojo-derived tool calls (5 seeds, 80/20), not the official end-to-end AgentDojo benchmark. Research prototype, draft paper.

09 · 45 s

An AI denies a loan. What's the smallest change that flips it?

My prototype CCTM (a Tsetlin Machine: a model made of rules you can read) searches for the smallest feature change that flips a loan decision, and writes a receipt that can be re-checked without the model. On synthetic loans with a built-in gender bias, training on one-feature "twins" cut the approval gap from 0.18 to 0.04 on average over 3 seeds; in one seed the twins barely helped.

The catch: the twins were labelled by the bias-free rule that generated the data, and real data has no such oracle. Synthetic data only; draft paper (v0.1), not peer reviewed.

10 · 41 s

Blocking an unsafe AI agent kills the task. Repair the action instead

A blocking guardrail stops an unsafe AI agent action, and the task dies with it. REINS, my runtime guard, tries the edits the deployment allows (add a missing step, swap in a safer tool, drop the call, ask a human) and applies the cheapest one that keeps the policy safe. On my synthetic benchmark (12 seeds, 60 honest and 60 attack tasks per seed) both stopped every attack; blocking finished 32.8% of the honest tasks within the rules, REINS 100%.

That 100% is a best case: the steps these tasks forget are exactly the ones REINS is allowed to add, and without the audit insert it finishes 66.9%. Synthetic tasks, not a live agent. Draft paper.
